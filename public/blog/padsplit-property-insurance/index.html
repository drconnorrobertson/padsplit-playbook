PadSplit Property Insurance: A Shared-Housing Policy Review

Published July 9, 2026 · Updated November 21, 2026 · PadSplit Playbook editorial guide

A shared-housing insurance review starts with the actual operation and the policy documents. “Landlord coverage,” “host protection” or a certificate of insurance is not a complete answer to whether a particular event is covered. Identify who is insured, which property and activities are described, and the conditions and exclusions that apply.

This original review process helps prepare a conversation with a licensed insurance professional. It does not recommend a universal liability limit, identify a policy suitable for every PadSplit property or promise that a claim will be paid. Policy wording, state rules and the facts of a loss matter.

Describe the operation before comparing quotes

Give the agent or insurer an accurate written description of the property’s use. Include the ownership and operating entities, address, room-rental arrangement, furnished areas, services, intended occupancy, typical stay pattern, renovation status and platform involvement. Describe your actual arrangements rather than assuming a weekly price means weekly resident turnover.

The NAIC’s home-sharing consumer guidance recommends discussing rental use with the agent or insurer and understanding the scope of platform-provided protection. The Texas Department of Insurance’s rental guidance similarly directs owners to ask whether an endorsement or separate policy fits the use. These are general home-sharing resources, not a determination that every co-living arrangement is a short-term rental or has the same insurance classification.

Ask the reviewer to confirm how the proposed policy treats the disclosed operation. Keep the submitted description and response with the final documents. If the operation changes, identify the process for updating the insurer rather than assuming the original description still fits.

Build a policy review worksheet

Request the declarations, relevant forms, endorsements and exclusions for the proposed coverage. Record the form identifier, provision and unresolved question. A quote comparison should show differences in coverage terms as well as premiums.

A row marked “unresolved” is a follow-up task, not a conclusion that coverage exists. Assign an owner and obtain the relevant document or explanation before relying on the proposal. Do not assume a larger limit resolves an exclusion, or that two similarly named products have identical terms.

Review platform documents as a separate file

If a platform advertises protection, obtain the current terms applicable to your arrangement. Identify the provider, eligible parties and events, exclusions, limits, prerequisites, documentation and notice process. Ask whether it is insurance, a contractual reimbursement program or another arrangement; do not infer that from the marketing label.

Have the insurance professional review how those documents interact with the proposed policy. This guide does not describe current PadSplit program benefits or exclusions. A generic statement that a platform covers member damage cannot establish what your current documents provide.

A fictional example: two quotes need different follow-ups

Suppose Quote A includes a building limit and refers to endorsements that have not been supplied. Quote B supplies its forms but uses a property-use description that differs from the owner’s actual room-rental arrangement. Neither file is ready for a reliable comparison simply because it contains a premium.

For A, request the missing forms. For B, correct the factual description and obtain the reviewer’s response. Then compare the resulting terms. This example uses no real insurer, price or coverage finding; it illustrates why a completeness check comes before a recommendation.

Do not turn a deductible multiple into a reporting rule

A rule such as “report only losses above two or three times the deductible” can conflict with notice or cooperation obligations. Review the actual policy and applicable requirements with the insurer or qualified adviser. A decision about paying an expense yourself is separate from whether an incident must be reported.

The Texas Department of Insurance’s claims guide advises prompt filing, documenting damage, preventing further damage and retaining expense receipts. Its state-specific deadlines should not be copied into a nationwide procedure. Maintain your policy’s reporting contacts and requirements before an incident, and seek appropriate emergency assistance when needed.

Preserve dated photos, relevant messages, maintenance records, estimates, receipts and the communications log. Record the instructions received and requests still outstanding. Documentation supports review; it does not guarantee a coverage outcome.

Keep the file current as the property changes

Use the actual renewal and policy dates, property changes and professional advice to set review points. A portfolio schedule should reconcile locations, insured interests, terms and reporting responsibilities. A particular property count does not by itself establish when a blanket policy or excess layer is appropriate.

Connect the approved insurance costs and obligations to the property underwriting model. Use the zoning and occupancy review for the separate land-use file and the maintenance-request guide for operational records. Insurance review does not replace those separate determinations.
