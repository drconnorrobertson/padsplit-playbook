Zoning and Occupancy Laws for PadSplit and Co-Living Rentals: What Operators Need to Know

Published August 20, 2026 · Updated November 21, 2026 · PadSplit Playbook editorial guide

A co-living review needs more than a bedroom count or a statement that “room rentals are allowed.” Identify the address, proposed use, rental arrangement and physical changes, then connect each conclusion to current source material. A platform listing category is not an address-specific zoning determination.

This guide organizes questions for the local authority and qualified advisers. It does not establish a lawful use, maximum occupancy, permit entitlement or enforceability of a private restriction for any property. The state examples below illustrate different legal questions; they are not a nationwide rule.

Define the exact proposal before asking whether it is allowed

Prepare a short project description with the parcel identifier, existing approved use, proposed room arrangement, intended number of residents, shared facilities, billing and stay arrangements, parking plan and proposed construction. Attach the existing floor plan and a marked proposed plan. Describe the actual operation rather than choosing the label that sounds easiest to approve.

Ask which definitions apply to that proposal: dwelling, household, sleeping unit, rooming or boarding house, lodging, co-living or another local category. Keep those definitions beside the use table and any conditions. A permission applying to one category does not answer the question for a different use.

Separate relationship-based limits from use and safety requirements

Rules about who may live together, where a use is permitted and what physical standards apply are different issues. State legislation can change one issue without resolving every other approval. Avoid converting a news headline about occupancy reform into an assumption that every proposed conversion is permitted.

Colorado: what the enacted occupancy measure addresses

The Colorado General Assembly’s enacted summary of HB24-1007 says the measure prohibits local governments from limiting people living together in a dwelling based on familial relationship. It preserves occupancy limits based on demonstrated health and safety standards or specified affordable-housing program guidelines. The summary identifies July 1, 2024 as its effective date. That is not a conclusion that a particular floor plan, use or private covenant is approved.

Washington: which lots the co-living provision covers

RCW 36.70A.535 requires cities and counties planning under that chapter to permit co-living on lots within an urban growth area that allow at least six multifamily residential units, including mixed-use lots. The statute defines co-living and addresses local implementation and conflicting regulations. Its six-unit condition is not a general permission to convert every single-family home.

The Washington Department of Commerce’s co-living resources include guidance, a model ordinance and a checklist. Guidance and models help frame questions; distinguish them from the governing law and the rules applicable to the actual parcel.

Build a review file that keeps each question separate

The following is an original organizational worksheet, not a list of universal legal requirements. Ask the relevant authority or adviser which items apply and what record supports the answer.

Give each unresolved issue an owner, the requested document or answer, and a follow-up date. Save the source link, version date, relevant section and the facts the reviewer considered. “Someone said it was fine” is not enough information for another person to reproduce the review.

Use written questions that expose assumptions

When contacting the planning or zoning office, provide the actual project description and ask: “Which use classification and provisions apply to this proposal at this parcel? What additional review or application is needed?” Ask whether the response addresses the room-rental arrangement and proposed physical changes, not merely the existing dwelling.

Record who responded, when, which facts were supplied and any limits on the response. Do not treat an informal conversation as the same thing as a permit or binding determination. If sources conflict, preserve both and ask a qualified local land-use professional what process resolves the conflict.

Review private restrictions separately. Do not assume municipal permission overrides every covenant, or that an association restriction is enforceable without analysis. Give the complete governing documents and proposed arrangement to the adviser instead of relying on a short resale summary or an assumption about the board’s preferences.

A fictional review example: permission still unresolved

Suppose an owner has a five-room proposal and a copy of a state reform announcement. The parcel’s use classification has not been confirmed, and the proposed plan differs from the existing approved plan. The review worksheet should record those two open questions. It should not record “five residents approved” based on the announcement.

Assign one follow-up to establish the applicable use and another to determine the plan-review process. Update the budget and schedule only when the relevant answers are supported. This example illustrates a documentation gap; it does not predict how any jurisdiction would decide the proposal.

Carry the supported conditions into the operating model

After the appropriate review, connect the supported configuration and conditions to the conversion scope, schedule and room-level model. If the assumptions change, reopen the affected questions rather than keeping an earlier conclusion attached to a different project.

Use the property underwriting guide and worked room-pricing and collections guide for the next calculations. A room count in a spreadsheet is an input to verify, not proof that the proposed operation is authorized.
