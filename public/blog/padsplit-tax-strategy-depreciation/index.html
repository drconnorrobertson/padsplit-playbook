PadSplit Tax Records: Depreciation, Cost Segregation and Loss Limits

Published July 23, 2026 · Revised November 22, 2026 · By Dr. Connor Robertson

A furnished room rental does not create an automatic W-2 offset. Before estimating an after-tax return, separate three questions: what costs belong to which assets or expenses, when deductions arise, and whether the owner can use a resulting loss. This guide organizes that review; it does not determine a taxpayer’s filing position or promise savings.

The records example below is entirely fictional. No property, invoice, return or participation history has been reviewed. The linked IRS publications were checked November 22, 2026; the editions available at those links are for 2025 returns. For a later return, confirm subsequent guidance and the rules applicable to its tax year.

Start with basis, use and the in-service date

Land is not depreciable. Residential rental buildings generally use 27.5-year recovery under the general depreciation system, with a mid-month convention; alternative-system rules can differ. Furniture is separately classified. Depreciation starts when property is ready and available for rental use, not simply when an invoice is paid. Personal use and conversion from personal to rental use can affect the calculation. See IRS Publication 527, depreciation and rental-use rules.

Keep the closing statement, acquisition documents, support for the land/building allocation, itemized purchases and evidence of readiness. Ask the preparer to reconcile the asset register to those records and the prior depreciation schedule. A list of cash payments alone does not establish a first-year deduction.

Cost segregation needs support for the actual components

A cost segregation study analyzes asset classifications and costs. It does not make every wall, plumbing line or furnishing immediately deductible. The IRS publishes Publication 5653, its Cost Segregation Audit Technique Guide, for examinations; the guide expressly is not a pronouncement of law or the IRS’s position. Its existence is not approval of a particular study or a guaranteed allocation.

Request an explanation of the method, source records, component descriptions and reconciliation to total basis. If a proposal offers a deduction percentage before reviewing the property, ask what evidence supports it. Compare the written scope and fee with the owner’s ability to use deductions and the expected holding period. This guide assigns no market-average study fee, reclassification percentage or payback threshold.

Check bonus depreciation by asset and dates

IRS Publication 946 explains the restored 100% special depreciation allowance for certain qualified property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025. Eligibility, acquisition rules, business use, exclusions and elections still matter. The residential building itself does not become eligible merely because separately identified shorter-life assets may qualify. Property acquired earlier can fall under different rules.

Preserve purchase contracts and the date each asset became ready for use. Ask the preparer to document the applicable recovery period, depreciation system, convention and election. Review state conformity separately. Accelerating a deduction changes timing; later disposition can produce depreciation recapture, as Publication 946 explains. A federal depreciation estimate is not a complete after-tax investment return.

Determine whether a loss can actually be used

Rental activities generally are passive. Real estate professional status requires both more than 750 qualifying service hours and more than half of the taxpayer’s personal services in qualifying real property businesses. Material participation in the rental activity is a separate requirement; grouping elections can matter. Nonpassive treatment does not remove every deduction limit. Publication 925 addresses these tests and the interaction with basis and at-risk limits.

A weekly payment schedule does not establish an average customer-use period of seven days or less. That rental-activity exception concerns actual use periods, and material participation remains relevant to passive treatment. Keep occupancy dates and records of work performed rather than inferring tax status from the platform’s billing cadence. Publication 925 explains the customer-use calculation.

The special rental-real-estate loss allowance also has conditions. The Form 8582 instructions describe active participation and an allowance of up to $25,000, generally reduced as modified adjusted gross income rises above $100,000 and eliminated at $150,000. Filing-status and other exceptions matter; these are not ordinary AGI shortcuts or a promise that any particular owner qualifies.

Work a fictional evidence packet

Suppose an operator hands the preparer the four records below. Each starts with an open question, not a deduction amount. Assign a person to obtain the missing evidence and retain the preparer’s conclusion separately. These are suggested review roles, not people already engaged for a real property.

T1 — Closing statement with one combined purchase figure

Evidence gap
No supported land/building allocation or prior-use history
Next request
Obtain acquisition documents, allocation support and prior-use facts

Bring a reconciled packet to the preparer

Include prior returns, depreciation schedules, suspended-loss records, ownership and financing documents, occupancy history, work records and planned disposition details. Separate ordinary operating payments, capital purchases, financing cash flows and reserve transfers in the ledger; have the preparer decide the tax treatment. A reserve transfer is not a substitute for an invoice showing a cost actually incurred.

Use the fictional rental cash comparison for the operating side and the conversion-budget guide for setup spending. Keep cash results and tax deductions in separate reconciliations.
